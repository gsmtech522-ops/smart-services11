How to Flash Stock Firmware Using ADB & Fastboot

Type
Step-by-Step Guide
Level
Advanced
Time
~30 minutes
Applies to
Pixel, Xiaomi, Motorola, OnePlus, Realme, Asus and other Fastboot-friendly devices
Last updated
YYYY-MM-DD

When an Android device gets stuck in a bootloop, suffers system corruption, or needs an official OS downgrade, flashing the stock firmware is the ultimate fix. While chipset-specific tools (SP Flash Tool, Odin) operate in low-level boot modes, Fastboot flashes partition binaries directly via the bootloader interface.

Reference

  1. 1. Requirements & prerequisites

    • Platform Tools (ADB & Fastboot) installed on your PC — see setup guide.
    • Unlocked bootloader on the target Android device (mandatory for Fastboot flashing).
    • Official stock firmware / factory image matching your exact model.

    Warranty & data loss: unlocking the bootloader wipes data and may void warranty on many phones. Back up IMEI, data and photos before starting.

  2. 2. Step 1 — Extract firmware images

    1. Download the correct stock firmware/factory package for your phone model.
    2. Extract the firmware archive (.zip or .tgz).
    3. Look for raw partition images: boot.img, recovery.img, system.img, vendor.img, vbmeta.img, super.img.
    4. Copy all .img files into your Platform Tools folder.

  3. 3. Step 2 — Boot into Fastboot mode

    1. Power off the phone completely.
    2. Hold Volume Down + Power until the Fastboot / Bootloader screen appears.
    3. Connect the phone to the PC via USB cable.
    4. Open CMD in your Platform Tools folder and verify:
    fastboot devices

    If your device serial appears, the connection is successful.

  4. 4. Step 3 — Flash security & critical boot partitions

    Flash bootloader, verification and kernel partitions first to prevent boot loops.

    Disable Android Verified Boot (AVB / VBMeta):

    fastboot flash vbmeta vbmeta.img --disable-verity --disable-verification

    Flash boot partition (kernel & ramdisk):

    fastboot flash boot boot.img

    Flash recovery partition (optional, if present):

    fastboot flash recovery recovery.img

    Flash vendor_boot (Android 12+ devices):

    fastboot flash vendor_boot vendor_boot.img

  5. 5. Step 4 — Flash system & OS partitions

    Depends on whether your phone uses standard partitions or dynamic partitions (Android 10+).

    Option A — Modern devices with dynamic partitions (super.img):

    fastboot flash super super.img

    Option B — Legacy devices with separate partitions:

    fastboot flash system system.img
fastboot flash vendor vendor.img
fastboot flash product product.img

  6. 6. Step 5 — Wipe data & reboot

    Prevent crashes by clearing old user data that may conflict with the new system:

    fastboot erase userdata
fastboot erase cache
fastboot reboot

    First boot: takes 5–10 minutes after a full flash. Do not disconnect or force reboot during the first boot.

  7. 7. Alternative — ADB sideload full OTA

    If your bootloader is locked or you don't want to extract images, flash a Full OTA zip via Recovery + ADB sideload.

    1. Download the Full OTA firmware package (.zip).
    2. Boot into Stock Recovery (Volume Up + Power).
    3. Select Apply update from ADB (or ADB Sideload).
    4. Connect the phone to the PC and verify:
    adb devices
# Should show: xxxxxx    sideload
    1. Send the firmware file:
    adb sideload official_firmware.zip
    1. Wait for 100%, then select Reboot System Now in recovery.

  8. 8. Troubleshooting & pro tips

    ProblemFix
    FAILED (remote: partition doesn't exist)Device uses A/B slots — try fastboot flash boot_a boot.img or switch slot: fastboot set_active a
    Stuck in Fastboot-D ModeNewer devices switch to FastbootD for dynamic partitions. Enter with: fastboot reboot fastboot
    First boot loopLet it run 10 minutes before force restarting.
    waiting for deviceInstall bootloader USB drivers (Google USB Driver or vendor driver).
    VBMeta flags rejectedUpdate Platform Tools — old fastboot.exe does not support AVB flags.
